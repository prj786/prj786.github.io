ewe — privacy policy

Last updated: 29 August 2026

ewe is a free, open-source desktop operating system. This policy covers the ewe desktop and its applications, including the optional Google sign-in.

The short version

ewe has no servers and collects nothing. Everything ewe does with your data happens on your own computer.

Google account (optional)

Connecting a Google account is entirely optional — until you do, no Google functionality appears anywhere in ewe. If you connect one, ewe requests access to:

  • your basic profile (name, email, picture) — shown in your own Settings;
  • your calendar (read-only) — shown in your own Quick Settings;
  • Gmail (read-only, optional) — unread count and subjects, shown on your own desktop;
  • hidden app storage on your Drive — to back up your settings file so a new machine can restore it;
  • your Drive files (optional) — only if you enable the “Google Drive” folder, which mounts them locally.

All of this data flows directly between your computer and Google. It is never sent to, stored by, or visible to the ewe project. Sign-in credentials (the OAuth refresh token) are stored only in your computer's encrypted keyring. Signing out revokes access and deletes local caches.

ewe's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What the ewe project sees

Nothing. No analytics, no telemetry, no accounts of ours. Package downloads hit GitHub's infrastructure (subject to GitHub's privacy statement); timezone auto-detection asks a geolocation service where your public IP is, sends nothing else, and can be disabled.
